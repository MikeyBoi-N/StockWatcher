How this works

This page does not predict a price. Each model draws hundreds of possible futures from what the market has done before, and the chart shows how they spread out. Where the cloud is dense, many futures passed through. The histogram on the right shows where they ended.

Random walk moves randomly at today's volatility. It knows nothing, which makes it the yardstick: a model that cannot beat it has not learned anything.

Historical bootstrap replays real past candles in short runs, resized from the volatility they happened at to today's. It keeps the market's real texture: wick sizes, sudden gaps, crash-sized moves, and calm and wild stretches that cluster. It has no view on direction.

Bootstrap, GJR-GARCH vol is the same replay with a fitted volatility model in place of the bootstrap's simple running average. A drop raises volatility more than a rise of the same size, and after a spike or a calm spell volatility drifts back toward the stock's normal level instead of staying where it last was. It is refitted at every forecast date from the candles before it.

GARCH + earnings gaps adds the one move that is on the calendar. Past earnings reactions (dated from the company's SEC filings) are taken out of the replay and the volatility fit, and on each scheduled release date every path takes one of the stock's own past earnings moves instead, resized to today's volatility. Volatility does not jump after the release the way it would after a surprise crash. In the backtest the release dates are the real ones, which companies announce weeks ahead.

Pattern analogs tests the idea that chart patterns are real. It finds the past moments whose last few candles (shape, wicks and volume, in volatility units) looked most like the last few now, and replays what happened after each one. Closer matches count for more. If patterns carry information, this model should beat the other two in the backtest.

Engine analogs (stocks only) uses this app's own analysis. It finds past weeks, across every tracked stock, when the scoring engine read a stock the way it reads this one now: the same Trend, Entry and Trade scores, market regime, RSI, stretch above the 50-day, distance to support and strength against SPY. It then replays what those stocks did next, resized to this stock's volatility.

Fitted signal (stocks only) is the historical bootstrap tilted by a fitted model's view of this stock. The model is a ridge regression trained on the replayed weeks of the broad list. It predicts each stock's next-month return against the benchmark from its engine scores and the known signals, using only weeks whose outcome had already happened. The tilt is usually a fraction of a percent a month, which is about the size of any real edge. A model that claims more is usually overfitted.

The backtest moves back through history, makes a forecast at each step using only the data before it, then checks what really happened. Skill compares forecast quality (CRPS, which rewards being both right and confident) against the random walk. The value after ± is one standard error, so a skill smaller than about twice that is noise. In 90% band should be near 90%: lower means the model is overconfident, higher means it is too timid. Direction calls counts the times at least 60% of paths agreed on a direction, and what following each call would have earned after costs. Costs are one round trip: the fee on both sides plus the spread and slippage. Stocks default to a commission-free broker with a 2 bps spread. Crypto defaults to Coinbase's entry-tier taker fee (about 0.60% per side; check your own tier) plus 5 bps, and shows the live quoted spread next to the input.

Compared with: plain SPY rewards simply holding riskier stocks. In a rising market, a stock that moves 1.5 times as much as the market beats SPY with no skill at all. Risk-adjusted subtracts the stock's beta times SPY's return. Beta is measured over the year before each reading and shrunk a third of the way toward 1 (Blume's adjustment), because a single year overstates how unusual a stock is. Sector peers also compares each stock with the others in its sector that week, so a sector's run (tech in 2023–2025) isn't credited to the stocks that happen to be in it. That's the strictest test of stock picking.

How returns are measured: total return including dividends (without them, a stock paying 6% a year looks half a percent a month worse than it did), bought at the close after the reading, since nobody can trade at the close the reading was computed from.

Known signals are price effects with published evidence behind them: 12-month momentum (skipping the latest month), last week's and last month's returns (short-term reversal), volatility, closeness to the 52-week high, distance from the 200-day average, RSI and unusual volume. Each is tested on its own, ranked against the other stocks each week. Testing twelve at once means one or two will look significant at two standard errors by luck alone, so a signal is only highlighted beyond three.

Options vs model volatility compares the move the options market is pricing for a stock (implied volatility from the latest data build) with the spread of the model's simulated paths. Options pricing in much more movement than the model expects often means options are expensive, and the reverse means they're cheap. The implied volatility is from the most recent build, at the option's own expiry, so treat it as a rough comparison.

Does the app's analysis work? replays the scoring engine on every past week of five years of prices, using only the data available that week, and checks what followed. vs SPY is the average return over SPY for stocks with each verdict. Rank correlation says whether higher scores lined up with better returns that week. 0 is no relationship and negative means the score ranked stocks backwards. Dates are spaced a full window apart, so each one is an independent test. The live record does the same with the verdicts the app actually published, fundamentals included. It is small for now and grows with every build.

Most likely path highlights one real simulated run: the one that stays in the most crowded part of the cloud from start to finish (at each step, how many other runs closed near it). Any single exact path is about as unlikely as any other, so this is the most typical run, not a prediction. It shows what an ordinary outcome looks like candle by candle, wicks and all, and it sits near the median by construction.

Reset to live forecast returns to the latest candles and the default inputs. It shows the historical bootstrap unless another model beat the random walk on this symbol by more than the noise. Picking whichever scored best would mostly pick whichever got lucky. The Reset beside the costs puts only that row (horizon, paths, pattern length, matches and costs) back to its defaults.

Show best fit for backtests every model at the current horizon, the pattern models at every pattern length and match count, and ranks them on the question you pick. Range (CRPS) asks how well the whole spread of outcomes matched what happened, which is mostly a question of volatility, so steady volatility models tend to win it. Direction (Brier) asks how well each model's chance of "up" matched whether the price rose. Models are ranked by skill minus two standard errors, the low end of what the skill could really be, so a noisy model that got lucky can't top the board; the top three are marked gold, silver and bronze on the model buttons, and hovering a model shows its numbers and how far it is behind the leader. Trying many settings still flatters the winner a little, so its skill is a best case.

Paper record: backtests on the same five years are exploratory, because every idea tried on them makes a lucky result likelier. The paper record writes hypotheses down first, with the exact measurement, stocks, benchmark, window, number of tests and pass bar, and scores them only on weeks that happen afterwards. Registrations are in data/paper/registry.json, added by node scripts/register_paper.mjs and never edited. The commit that adds one is its timestamp. If the engine, a signal or the measurement code changes after registration, the hypotheses that depend on it are marked "rules changed" rather than quietly scoring something else.

Days ago (the arrows and slider under the chart) rewinds the starting point by up to 30 candles so you can watch a past forecast play out against the candles that actually came next.

Crypto candles come live from Coinbase. Stock candles are five years of daily candles from this site's data build, updated with each build.

Technical reference

Written for quantitatively literate readers and for AI agents. It states exactly what every number on this page is, so nothing has to be inferred. Source of truth: js/simulator.js, js/replay.js, js/signals.js, js/paper.js, js/engine.js, scripts/forecast_vol.mjs. Nothing here is investment advice, and no model or signal on this page has a demonstrated out-of-sample edge unless the paper record says it passed.

Conventions

  • All forecasts are distributions, never point predictions. Returns are log returns relative to the previous close unless stated. Percent figures labelled "skill" are relative improvements over the random walk, not returns.
  • Everything is causal: a forecast or reading at origin t uses candles 0..t only. Backtests replay past origins under the same rule. Randomness is a seeded mulberry32 generator, so identical inputs reproduce identical paths.
  • Stocks use daily candles: five years from the site's data build (data/replay.json: OHLCV, a dividend-adjusted close, weekly engine-input rows, and earnings-reaction sessions dated from SEC EDGAR filings). If the replay file is missing, the last 120 closes in data/market.json are used, with no wicks or volume. Crypto uses up to 3,000 live Coinbase Exchange candles at the chosen size.
  • Universes are survivor lists: "App's list" is the hand-picked tracked set (leans to past winners); "+ S&P 100" adds today's index members. Neither contains delisted or collapsed stocks.

Series preparation

  • Each candle i becomes rel = log(o, h, l, c / ci−1). The volatility forecast for candle i, made at the close of i−1, is an EWMA with λ = 0.94: σ²i+1 = λσ²i + (1−λ)·r²i.
  • Volume feature: log(vi / median of the previous 50 volumes), clamped to ±3.

Models

  • Random walk (baseline): Gaussian log returns at σt+1 with drift −σ²/2 (a martingale in price), and synthetic wicks of 0.4σ|N(0,1)| beyond the body.
  • Historical bootstrap (filtered historical simulation): the candle library is standardized by its own σi, recentred by the mean standardized close so past drift is not replayed, then sampled as a stationary block bootstrap (mean block length 8, restart probability 1/8). Each draw is rescaled to the path's current σ, and σ is updated along the path by the same EWMA.
  • Bootstrap, GJR-GARCH vol: the same replay with GJR-GARCH(1,1) volatility, σ²t+1 = ω + (α + γ·1[rt<0])·r²t + β·σ²t. Fitted by Gaussian quasi-maximum likelihood on demeaned close returns with variance targeting (ω = σ̄²(1 − α − γ/2 − β), σ̄² the sample variance), using Nelder-Mead from (α, γ, β) = (0.05, 0.05, 0.85) under α, γ, β ≥ 0 and α + γ/2 + β < 0.999. It is refitted at every origin, and needs 120 closes; with fewer it falls back to the bootstrap.
  • GARCH + earnings gaps: past earnings-reaction sessions are removed from both the library and the GARCH likelihood. On each scheduled release step inside the horizon, every path draws one past reaction, rescaled to the path's σ, and the variance update skips that step. It needs at least 4 past reactions. In backtests the release dates are the real ones; companies announce them weeks ahead.
  • Pattern analogs: compares the last w candles (w ∈ {6, 12, 24, 48}) with every earlier window whose next H candles are known at the origin. Distance is Euclidean over standardized close, high and low moves, plus 0.25 × the squared volume-feature difference, weighted by recency ((0.5 + 0.5(k+1)/w)²). The top min(matches, n/3) windows are kept and weighted with a Gaussian kernel whose bandwidth is the median kept distance. Each path replays one match's next H candles, scaled by σnow / σmatch.
  • Engine analogs (stocks): the same kernel replay, but matched on an 8-dimensional engine state across all replayed stocks' weekly readings: Trend/100, Entry/100, Trade/100, regime level (1, 0.66, 0.33, 0), RSI/100, stretch above the 50-day in monthly σ (clamped ±3, /3), distance to entry support in weekly σ (clamped 0..4, /4), and 3-month relative strength in σ√3 units (clamped ±3, /3). Only readings whose H-session outcome had fully elapsed before the origin are eligible.
  • Fitted signal (stocks, not ETFs): the bootstrap with a per-candle log drift equal to the ridge model's predicted 21-session excess return ÷ 21. The model is refitted as of the origin, using only weeks whose outcome window had ended.

Chart and statistics

  • Density: each simulated candle spreads one unit of mass uniformly over its [low, high] across 220 price rows per step, blurred vertically with triangular weights over ±3 rows; opacity = (mass / max)0.55. Bands: the 5–95% and 25–75% quantiles of simulated closes per step. The dashed lines are the 5%, 50% and 95% quantiles.
  • Most likely path: the simulated run that maximizes the sum, over steps, of the log local density of its close (the count of all paths' log closes within one Silverman bandwidth, 1.06 · IQR/1.349 · n−1/5), with near-ties broken toward the median. It is a typical run, not a mode of the path distribution and not a prediction.
  • Paths ending higher: the share of terminal simulated closes above the origin close. Options vs model volatility: the latest build's at-the-money implied volatility against the annualized standard deviation of simulated terminal log returns, √(252/H) × sd. It is shown only at the live origin, since the quote is today's.

Backtest ("Did the forecasts hold up?")

  • Origins run back from n−1−H in steps of H (non-overlapping outcome windows), at most 120, each with at least max(w+H+20, 40) candles of history. Every model simulates 200 paths with seed 7 + origin.
  • Range skill: CRPS of the terminal log return (sample estimator), as mean(CRPSRW − CRPSmodel) / mean(CRPSRW), with a paired standard error sd(diff)/√n / mean(CRPSRW). CRPS rewards calibrated width more than direction, so volatility models tend to win it. Direction skill: the same construction on the Brier score of P(closet+H > closet).
  • Coverage: the shares of outcomes inside the 50% and 90% bands, and below 5% / above 95% separately. A direction call is made when at least 60% of paths agree; it is filled at the next close, held to t+H, and charged one round trip (2 × fee + spread and slippage, in bps).
  • Green or red means more than 2 standard errors from zero. With few tests (under 30) every figure is mostly noise.

Model selection

  • Reset to live forecast: shows the bootstrap unless some model's range skill − 2·SE > 0 over at least 10 tests; then the highest skill among those.
  • Show best fit for: backtests a grid (pattern analogs at 4 pattern lengths × 3 match counts, engine analogs at 3 match counts, every other available model once) against the random walk. Each model keeps its best setting by the lower bound L = skill − 2·SE for the chosen objective (range or direction), and models are ranked by L. Gold, silver and bronze mark the top three. Taking the best of a grid is a selection, so the winner's skill is optimistically biased; L limits but does not remove that.

Engine scorecard ("Does the app's analysis work?")

  • The real scoring engine is re-run on each past week's price-only inputs. Fundamentals, valuation, analyst data, options and earnings dates cannot be reconstructed point-in-time, so Quality is null, Trade is only its price component, "strong" uses the ETF rule, and earnings are assumed never to be within 14 days. This tests the engine's price logic, not the full engine.
  • Outcome: the total (dividend-adjusted) return from the close 1 session after the reading (entry lag), held h ∈ {5, 21, 63} sessions. Benchmarks: SPY (1+r)/(1+m) − 1; risk-adjusted r − β·m, with β a 252-day OLS beta against SPY shrunk one third toward 1 (Blume), needing 120 shared days and otherwise 1; sector peers the risk-adjusted excess minus that week's sector mean (sectors with fewer than 3 stocks are pooled).
  • Test dates are thinned to one per ⌈h/5⌉ weeks, counted from the newest complete date, so outcome windows never overlap. Each statistic is a per-date cross-sectional mean, then mean ± standard error across dates; dates are the independent unit.
  • Signals: 12 (the four engine scores plus 12-month momentum skipping a month, last month's and last week's returns, 30-day volatility, closeness to the 52-week high, distance above the 200-day, RSI(14), and volume against its 20-day average). Each is ranked within the week and scaled to [−√3, √3] (unit variance, ties averaged). Rank correlation is the per-date Spearman correlation with excess return; spread is the top-quintile minus bottom-quintile mean excess (at least 10 stocks). Single signals are judged at ±3 standard errors, roughly Bonferroni for 12 tests.
  • Fitted model: ridge regression of the excess return (winsorized at each week's 5th/95th percentile and demeaned within the week) on the 11 rank features (Overall is excluded as a blend), with penalty 0.05 × rows and at least 52 training weeks. Walk-forward: before each test date it is refitted only on weeks whose entire outcome window, entry lag included, had ended.
  • Charts: dot = mean; line = ±2 SE (±3 SE for single signals); line and dot colour encode how far the estimate leans and whether it clears that band. The gauge plots the Buy-zone mean return on a fixed ±2.5 / 5 / 10% scale for 1 week / 1 month / 3 months, with gaps at ±2 SE. The sparkline is the running mean of the walk-forward top-minus-bottom spread, ending at the card's value. The ring fills a dot only for signals that cleared ±3 SE. The calendar marks published market days, green once at least h sessions old.

Live record and paper record

  • Live record: the verdicts the app actually published (one Firestore snapshot per data build, fundamentals included), using each market day's last build, scored with replay closes against the same benchmarks, with days thinned at least h sessions apart.
  • Paper record (data/paper/registry.json, append-only): hypotheses fixed before their test data exists, scored only on weeks after registeredOn. One-sided pass rule z ≥ 2.25 (5% Bonferroni across 4 hypotheses) once minDates independent tests exist. Each registration stores 32-bit FNV-1a fingerprints of the engine rules and scoring functions, the measurement code and each signal definition; any mismatch marks the dependent hypotheses "Rules changed" rather than scoring altered code. The frozen H2 weights also drive the main page's Signal column, the Strategy's expected value and the API's signal field. That use is unproven until H2 passes.

Volatility forecast (build time)

  • scripts/forecast_vol.mjs fits GJR-GARCH(1,1) to each stock's full replay history, with earnings reactions excluded, and averages the variance forecast E[σ²t+k] = σ̄² + φk−1(σ²t+1 − σ̄²), φ = α + γ/2 + β, over 21 sessions and over the quoted option's life (DTE × 252/365). Each scheduled release in the window adds (mean squared past reaction − σ̄²).
  • Validation on 2,510 replay forecasts: mean realized ÷ forecast variance 0.993 (unbiased), QLIKE 0.22 against 0.42 for 21-day realized volatility, which under-forecasts variance by about 40%. It is used by the engine's options-vehicle rule (IV ÷ forecast: ≤ 0.9 cheap, ≥ 1.3 expensive) and by the Strategy's expected value. Whether it beats implied volatility is untested until enough IV history accumulates.

Limitations an agent must not ignore

  • Survivorship bias in both universes; price-only replay; costs are assumptions (stocks 0 bps fee + 2 bps spread per round trip by default, crypto 60 bps per side + 5 bps); market holidays are not modelled in weekday counts; CRPS and Brier score terminal outcomes only, not paths; the rewind control reaches 30 candles, so a rewound chart is one test, not evidence.
  • "Within noise" means the data cannot distinguish the effect from zero. Do not describe it as a weak edge. Treat any claim that a model, signal or verdict predicts returns as unsupported unless its paper-record hypothesis shows "Passed".
  • Every figure here is recomputed in the browser from the current settings and data build. For machine use, prefer the JSON API (api/v1/), whose fields are documented in the repository README.
